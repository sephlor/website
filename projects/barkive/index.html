🐕 Barkive

Work in progress · v1

Visit barkive.ai View on GitHub →

I wanted both a lasting record of how much my dog barks and live remote access while I'm out. Instead of starting from an existing NVR like Frigate, I built the detection pipeline from scratch around Google's YAMNet sound classifier, so I understood every trade-off in it.

The camera's audio runs through YAMNet, which recognizes barks along with yips, howls, growls, and whimpers, and scores each one for volume and confidence. Every event is timestamped and saved, and the dashboards are interactive. It also listens for the doorbell chime in that same audio and marks it on the timeline, so a barking spell can be tied back to someone at the door. That part is still under development and throws the occasional false positive.

I intentionally keep recording sensitivity and display sensitivity separate. Everything is recorded at a low threshold and the browser filters the view, so I can always look stricter without ever losing data.

Everything lives on a Mac at home. Detection runs offline in near-real-time, and both the live camera view and the history are served through a Cloudflare Tunnel.

Raw events are only half the story — a day can hold hundreds of them. The history view rolls them up two ways so a whole day reads at a glance.

Barking activity counts barks per minute across the day and follows whatever window the timeline is zoomed to. Quiet stretches flatten out and the real flare-ups stand up as spikes, so it's obvious when the barking actually happened — the dashed markers line up with detected doorbell rings.

Bark spurts group the day into bursts — five or more barks with no gap longer than 30 seconds — and rate each one minor, moderate, or severe by how long it ran. It turns a wall of individual events into a short list of episodes I can actually scan: when it started, how long it lasted, how many barks, and what kind. Tapping a row zooms the timeline to that burst.
